# One Asset, Twelve Placements: We Ran Our Own System on Pillar #1

*By Sunny Patel. Last updated 23 July 2026.*

Every "repurpose your content into 12 formats" post has the same flaw. It never shows you the 12 formats.

So here they are. Twelve finished, paste-ready placements, all cut from one asset: our pillar, [How to Market a Website in 2026](/how-to-market-a-website/) — roughly 5,000 words, a four-step framework, and one number that's ours (median citation share of 8% across 50 sites). This page is the receipt for the claim the [Content Repurposing System](/distribution/repurposing/) page makes. That page describes the system. This page *is* the system, run once, in public.

One thing before you scroll. Repurposing is extraction, not compression. Every placement below carries **one** idea from the pillar. None of them summarise all four steps. A summary is a twelfth-generation photocopy — grey, soft, pointless. An extraction is a blade: one edge, sharp, does one job. Eleven of these twelve are extractions. The one exception is the talk, and I'll tell you why when we get there.

Now count the right-hand column, because it's the whole argument of this pack. Twelve placements, cut from just eight sections — and **three ideas** doing almost all the lifting: the 8% stat (pieces 2, 8, 12), "fix before you amplify — the order is the strategy" (pieces 1, 5, 6, 9), and "six numbers, not forty" (pieces 3, 10).

That repetition isn't padding. It's the mechanism. One idea, wearing twelve outfits, showing up on every surface a buyer or a model might be reading. Mention frequency is a visibility lever, not a sin — [Ahrefs found brand-mention count correlated with AI answer appearance more than almost any other factor](/how-to-market-a-website/#digital-pr-that-earns-citations-not-just-links). Saying "8%" in three places is the point.

Here's the verdict, plainly. **A pillar needs three or four extractable ideas to feed twelve placements. If yours has fewer than three, repurposing isn't your problem — the pillar is.** Don't run this system on a thin asset. You'll just distribute the thinness twelve times.

> **How to reuse this section.** Copy the table structure into your own SOP. Fill the "one idea extracted" column *before* you write a single placement. If you can't fill twelve rows from three or four distinct ideas, stop — the asset isn't ready to repurpose. The extraction map is the go/no-go gate, not a formality.

## Placement 1: LinkedIn carousel — the four-step order

The carousel earns its slots because the framework is inherently sequential, and a swipe *is* a sequence. One step per slide, the order enforced by the format. Eight slides.

```
SLIDE 1 (hook)
Most website marketing advice is written for a site
that launched yesterday.

Yours didn't.

SLIDE 2
You have history. Indexed pages. Rankings you forgot about.
And at least one conversion leak you don't know about yet.

"21 ways to promote your website" wasn't written for you.

SLIDE 3 — MEASURE
Step 1. Measure.

Baseline what you have before you touch anything.
Six numbers. Not a 40-tab dashboard.
You can't prove marketing worked if you never wrote down
where you started.

SLIDE 4 — FIX
Step 2. Fix.

Plug the leaks before you buy more water.
Technical debt and conversion leaks first.
Pouring traffic into a broken funnel is a subsidy for failure.

SLIDE 5 — STRENGTHEN
Step 3. Strengthen.

Make the site worth citing.
Prune the dead pages. Build on what you already rank for.
Publish what the models can't already say.

SLIDE 6 — AMPLIFY
Step 4. Amplify.

Distribute a site that's finally worth distributing.
Digital PR, email, communities, repurposing.
Last. On purpose.

SLIDE 7 (the turn)
Notice the order.

The steps everyone starts with — distribution, visibility —
come last.
The boring ones come first.

The order isn't a suggestion. The order is the strategy.

SLIDE 8 (CTA)
The whole playbook, in order, is one link away.

Measure. Fix. Strengthen. Amplify.
On the site you already have.

→ Full framework in the comments.
```

> **Adaptation notes.** Carousel copy, not prose — each slide is one thought sized for a phone screen (aim under 30 words per slide, hook slide under 15). The pillar's four `<h2>` steps map one-to-one onto slides 3–6; slide 7 is the pillar's "the order is the strategy" line, which is the asset's spine. No links on any slide except the CTA pointer on slide 8; the actual URL goes in the first comment (LinkedIn suppresses reach on in-body links). Build as a native document post or a 1080×1080 image set with the text baked in — do not rely on alt text to carry slide copy.

## Placement 4: Reddit answer (r/SEO) — measure before you touch anything

Prompt it answers: *"My traffic is down 40% and I don't know why. What do I do first?"*

The idea extracted: before you fix anything, baseline it. Reddit punishes self-promotion, so this answer is complete without the click and carries no link in the body.

```
Don't fix anything yet. I know that's the opposite of what you
want to hear when traffic's down 40%, but hear me out.

The mistake almost everyone makes here is jumping straight to
fixes — rewriting pages, chasing a Core Web Vitals score,
rebuilding the nav — before they know what actually dropped.
Then a month later they can't tell which change did what, or
whether anything did.

So first, measure. Specifically:

- Search Console, last 16 months. Is the drop clicks or
  impressions? Clicks down but impressions flat means a CTR or
  SERP-feature problem. Both down means a ranking or indexing
  problem. Very different fixes.

- Is it sitewide or one section? Sort the Pages report by
  clicks and compare periods. A 40% sitewide drop and a 40%
  drop concentrated in one template are completely different
  investigations.

- Did it happen on a date or over weeks? A cliff usually means
  a technical event (migration, deindex, manual action, a
  botched release). A slow slide usually means competition, an
  algo update, or content decay.

- Check the boring stuff that causes 40% cliffs: robots.txt
  changes, a noindex shipped by accident, canonical tags
  pointing somewhere dumb, a CDN update that started blocking
  crawlers. I've seen all four take a site down overnight.

Write those numbers down before you change a single thing.
That baseline is the only way you'll prove your fix worked
instead of arguing about it later.

Once you know what dropped and when, the fix is usually
obvious and small. It's the diagnosis people skip, not the fix.

If it's a date-based cliff, start with indexing and crawl.
If it's a slow slide, start with your top pages and whether
they've gone stale versus what's now ranking above you.
```

> **Adaptation notes.** Full answer, zero links in the body. Reddit's usable ratio is roughly **one link per ten substantive comments** — earn the account first, link almost never. The answer must resolve the question completely so it reads as help, not bait; the only "promotion" is that the profile bio links the site. This is not scheduled — it's pre-staged and posted only when a genuine matching thread appears. The community rules and the "be useful for weeks before you link once" method live in [Community-Led Distribution](/distribution/community-led/).

**Where the link goes:** Nowhere in the body. Profile bio only.

## Placement 5: Reddit answer (r/Entrepreneur) — the sequence, in second person

Prompt it answers: *"How do I actually market my existing website in 2026?"*

Same framework as piece 1, but rewritten as a war story in second person — Reddit rewards scars over frameworks.

```
You already have the hard part: a site that exists, with
history. That changes everything, because most advice out
there is written for someone launching next Tuesday. You're
not launching. You're fixing.

Here's the order I'd run it in, and the order matters more
than any single tactic.

First, you measure. Before you touch anything. You pull six
numbers — organic clicks, AI referrals, citation share,
conversion rate by page, indexed-vs-valuable pages, revenue
per visit — and you write them down. I've watched teams spend
a whole quarter "improving SEO" with no baseline, then argue
for a month about whether it worked. Don't be that team.

Then you fix what leaks. Not the fun stuff. The bucket. Your
homepage, your pricing page, your top landing page, your
forms, your checkout. If a stranger can't tell what you do in
five seconds, you're losing people before marketing even
starts. Pouring traffic into that is paying to lose faster.

Only then do you strengthen. You go through every page and
give it one of four verdicts: prune, merge, refresh, or leave
alone. I once deleted 400 pages from a site and watched
traffic go up. The client went pale at the graph, then very
happy. Fewer strong pages beat a big pile of thin ones, for
Google and for the AI engines both.

And last — last — you amplify. PR, email, communities,
repurposing. Because now you're promoting something that
actually holds water, so every visit is worth more.

The whole thing is four words in order: measure, fix,
strengthen, amplify. Everyone wants to start at amplify
because it's the fun one. Starting there is the single most
expensive mistake I see.

Do it backwards from how it feels. That's the trick.
```

> **Adaptation notes.** Second-person, war-story register — the pillar's first-person scars ("I deleted 400 pages", "went pale at the graph") are preserved because lived detail is what Reddit upvotes and what LLMs treat as information gain. Zero links in body; the four-step spine is the value. Post only into a real, matching thread. Keep it under ~2,500 characters so it reads as a comment, not an essay dump.

**Where the link goes:** Nowhere in the body. Profile bio only.

## Placement 6: Newsletter excerpt — the leaky-site argument

A 350-word issue lead. The idea: amplifying a leaky site is paying to lose faster — which is also the exact argument the email-moat page makes about why you capture first.

```
Subject line option: You're paying to lose faster

Here's an uncomfortable one for a Monday.

If your website converts badly and you spend money driving
traffic to it, you are not "investing in growth." You're
paying full price to expose your cracks to more people. Faster.

I call it amplifying a leaky site, and it's the most common
expensive mistake I see. The logic feels right — more traffic,
more sales — so people pour budget into ads, PR, and posting
five times a day, all pointed at a funnel that loses most of
who arrives.

Think about a bucket with a hole in it. Turning up the tap
doesn't fix the bucket. It just makes the puddle bigger and
your water bill higher.

The fix is boring and it's an order.

You measure what you've got. You plug the leaks — the
homepage nobody can parse in five seconds, the pricing page
hidden behind "contact us", the checkout that surprises people
with costs at the last step. Then, and only then, you turn up
the volume.

There's a version of this that applies to your email list too,
and it's the reason this newsletter exists. Most sites bolt a
popup onto the homepage and call it capture. But the intent
isn't on your homepage. It's on your best content and your top
landing pages — the places people arrive already interested.
Capture there, and you're building the one channel no
algorithm sits between you and. Google can bury you. ChatGPT
can answer instead of sending the click. Your list opens the
email you sent, when you sent it, with nothing in the middle.

A 2,000-person list that opens beats 50,000 visitors who
bounce. Every time. Because you can reach the list on purpose.

So before you spend a penny amplifying: is the bucket fixed?

If you're not sure, that's your answer. Measure first.

— Sunny
```

> **Adaptation notes.** ~350-word issue lead written to run as the top of a newsletter, not a standalone blog post — the sign-off and subject line are part of the deliverable. It carries the pillar's "amplifying a leaky site" idea *and* doubles as the capture argument, which is why the link points at the email-moat page rather than the pillar: this excerpt is the same case that [Email as a Distribution Moat](/distribution/email-moat/) makes at length. One link, in the footer, not inline.

**Where the link goes:** `/distribution/email-moat/` — footer.

## Placement 7: Talk / webinar outline — the whole framework

This is the one placement where summary is allowed. A 30-minute talk earns the right to walk all four steps, because the format gives you the time the others don't. Everywhere else, summary is banned. Here it's the format's whole job.

Fifteen slides, title plus one speaker beat each. Beats, not prose — you fill them live.

```
1.  Title: How to Market a Website You Already Have
    Beat: "Most talks on this are for people launching. This one isn't."

2.  The 21-channel lie
    Beat: Show a stock "21 ways to promote your site" listicle. Ask who has time for 21 of anything.

3.  You are not a new site
    Beat: You have history, indexed pages, a leak you don't know about. That's an advantage, treated right.

4.  The one idea of this talk
    Beat: Four steps, one order — measure, fix, strengthen, amplify. The order is the strategy.

5.  Step 1: Measure
    Beat: Six numbers, not forty. Name them fast, promise the list in the follow-up.

6.  The number that stops the room
    Beat: 50 sites, median 8% citation share, before any GEO work. Let it land. Ask the room to guess theirs.

7.  Step 2: Fix
    Beat: Plug the leaks before you buy more water. The bucket metaphor. Five money pages.

8.  Speed scores are a hobby
    Beat: Two seconds off checkout beats a 40-to-95 score on a blog post. Rank fixes by money.

9.  Step 3: Strengthen
    Beat: Four verdicts — prune, merge, refresh, leave. The 400-deleted-pages story goes here.

10. Publish what the models can't say
    Beat: Original data, named positions, first-person scars. The only durable moat.

11. One discipline, not two
    Beat: SEO and GEO share 80% of inputs. Stop paying twice for the same work.

12. Step 4: Amplify
    Beat: Now — and only now. Digital PR, email, communities, repurposing. Every visit is worth more.

13. The five expensive mistakes
    Beat: All the same mistake wearing different clothes — doing things out of order.

14. What to do Monday
    Beat: Run the 90-minute audit. Six numbers, 20 prompts, five money pages.

15. Close + one CTA
    Beat: "Measure. Fix. Strengthen. Amplify. On the site you already have." One link on screen.
```

> **Adaptation notes.** Slide title + one speaker beat each — a skeleton to present from, not a script to read. Fifteen slides suits a 25–30 minute slot with Q&A. This is the **only** placement in the pack permitted to summarise all four steps; the 30-minute runtime earns the breadth that would make a LinkedIn post or a tweet mush. Slide 6 is the anchor moment — hold the silence after the 8% number. Keep the pillar URL to a single closing slide.

**Where the link goes:** `/how-to-market-a-website/` — closing slide only.

## The run sheet

Twelve placements is a fortnight of work if you sequence it, or a month of chaos if you don't. Here's how a two-person team (Sunny plus one) actually ships this pack.

| Week | What ships | Who | Notes |
|------|-----------|-----|-------|
| Week 1 | Extraction map + pieces 1, 2, 4, 5 | Sunny writes 1, 2, 4, 5 | The voice-heavy ones — carousel, the confession post, both Reddit answers |
| Week 1 | Pieces 3, 6 | The +1 drafts from the map | Numbers thread and newsletter lead — structured enough to draft off the map |
| Week 2 | Pieces 7, 8, 9, 10 | Split: Sunny 8–10 scripts, +1 builds 7 | Three videos share one scaffold; talk outline is skeleton work |
| Week 2 | Pieces 11, 12 + publish cadence set | +1 drafts, Sunny approves | Quote graphics + podcast pitch, then load the drip |

**The drip schedule: twelve placements over 21 days. Drip, never dump.** Two hard rules:

- Never two placements on the same channel in the same week. Three videos over three weeks, not three in a day.
- Reddit answers (pieces 4 and 5) are **not scheduled**. They're pre-staged and posted only when a real, matching thread appears. A canned Reddit answer dropped into a dead thread is worse than no answer.

**The effort claim, numbered, because it's the whole pitch:**

1. The extraction map takes **90 minutes.** It's the only genuinely hard thinking, and you do it once.
2. Once the map exists, the **median piece takes 40 minutes.** You're not deciding what to say — the map already decided. You're just fitting one idea to one format.
3. The **whole pack is under two working days** of focused effort across the two of you.

That ratio is the entire argument for repurposing as a system. The thinking was already paid for when you wrote the pillar. The pack just spends it twelve times.

> **How to reuse this section.** Steal the two-column split: one person takes the voice-dependent placements (carousel, confessional posts, community answers), the other takes the structure-dependent ones (threads, scripts, one-pagers) they can build straight off the map. The 90-minute map is the shared input; everything downstream parallelises.

## FAQ

### Do all twelve placements suit every asset?

No. Twelve is the ceiling, not the quota. Ship a minimum of eight, and if you're cutting, drop video before Reddit — a community answer in the right thread outperforms a polished video nobody asked for. Match placements to where your buyers already are, not to a round number. An asset that only supports six honest placements should ship six.

### How long does one repurposing pack take?

Under two working days for a two-person team. The extraction map is 90 minutes of real thinking; once it exists, the median placement takes about 40 minutes because the map already decided what each one says. The thinking was paid for when you wrote the source asset — the pack just spends it across twelve surfaces.

### Won't twelve placements cannibalise the pillar?

No, because extractions link *in* and summaries compete *out*. Each placement here carries one idea and points back to the pillar for the rest, so it feeds the asset rather than replacing it. The thing that cannibalises is a summary — a mini-version of the whole page that ranks against it. Don't publish those. Extract, don't summarise.

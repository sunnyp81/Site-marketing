## 1. The Wedge: Do Not Launch a Podcast. Guest on 18.

**Podcast guesting is borrowing an established audience instead of building one from zero — and earning the brand mentions and indexed transcripts that AI answer engines learn from. You appear on shows that already have listeners, show-notes pages that already get crawled, and hosts whose authority already transfers to you.**

The reflex advice is "start a podcast." Don't. A new show's median episode does roughly 30 downloads in its first week — the number you need to beat 50% of all podcasts is in the low thirties ([Buzzsprout global stats](https://www.buzzsprout.com/global_stats)). Launching into that void is months of production for an audience you don't have. Guesting inverts it: one booking on a mid-tier marketing or SEO show puts you in front of an audience that already exists — on the order of 3,000–20,000 listeners per episode (an estimate; most shows don't publish audited figures, see §9) — plus a transcript that outlives the audio.

The currency of this kit is a number, not a bio. Every pitch leads with the 8% pilot median — **the median established site is cited in just 8% of the AI answers about its own topics** (50-site pilot; ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini; before any [GEO](/glossary/geo/) work). Bios get archived. Numbers get booked.

**TL;DR — three assets in this file:**
1. **Targets** — 18 vetted shows in three tiers (§3), booking order specified.
2. **Template** — one data-led pitch email, annotated line by line (§4).
3. **Angles** — three talking points a producer can lift straight into show prep (§5).

### Why guest instead of launch (the math)

| | Launch your own podcast | Guest on 18 |
|---|---|---|
| Time to first 1,000 listeners | Months to years — median episode ≈ 30 downloads in week one | One mid-tier booking |
| Effort per earned mention | ~8 hrs to produce one episode = **1 asset** | ~1 hr per spot = **4 assets** (below) |
| Transcript / citation surface | You own it — but nobody retrieves a show with no audience | The host's already-indexed show notes **plus** your re-hosted transcript |
| Who compounds | You — starting from zero authority | You — borrowing the host's authority, keeping the transcript |

**One guest spot = four assets, in one hour of effort:** (1) an audio mention in front of a borrowed audience, (2) a show-notes backlink from an indexed page, (3) a transcript you re-host as canonical text LLMs can retrieve, and (4) repurposing feedstock (clips, quotes, a newsletter issue). Producing your own episode buys you one asset for roughly eight times the effort.

The mechanism is earned mentions. **Ahrefs found the number of times a brand is mentioned across the web correlated more strongly with its appearance in AI answers than almost any other factor** ([Ahrefs, 2025](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). Guesting manufactures mentions in sources the models read — which is the whole point of [digital PR for citations](/distribution/digital-pr/).

> **Prerequisite — you need the number before you pitch.** The entire pitch engine is "your 8% stat." If Sunny is pitching site.marketing's own data, it exists. Anyone reusing this kit for a client must run the 20-prompt audit first, or the template has nothing to lead with: **[the 90-minute audit / 20-prompt method](/strategy/audit/)**. No original data, no pitch — a bio pitch converts at the under-5% rate this kit assumes (working target, §4).

## 4. The Pitch Template

**Register exception: the email below is Sunny's, first-person, and ships under his name.** The rules and annotation around it stay neutral-doc.

Merge fields: `{{host_first_name}}`, `{{show}}`, `{{episode}}` (a specific, named recent episode — never "your show" or "your great audience").

```
Subject: Median site shows up in 8% of AI answers — data from 50 audits

Hi {{host_first_name}},

I ran a 20-prompt AI-visibility audit on 50 established sites — ChatGPT,
Perplexity, Gemini, before any GEO work. Median citation share: 8%. Most
owners guess zero or fifty. Both wrong.

{{episode}} tells me your audience is already asking the question this data
answers.

Three things I can defend on air, no fluff:
- how the median site is invisible to AI, and how to measure your own number
  in 90 minutes
- why SEO and GEO are one job, not two retainers
- why amplifying a leaky site is paying to lose faster

The number's mine, from an ongoing 50-site pilot — not a vendor survey.

Worth a 30-minute recording?

Sunny
```

*(113 words in the body — under the 120 ceiling.)*

### Anatomy — why each line is there

| Line | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| Subject: the stat, not the ask | A number in the subject gets opened; "I'd love to come on your show" gets archived |
| Line 1 — the finding | Leads with the defensible original data (8%, 50 sites, 3 engines, 20-prompt method) — earns the read in one sentence |
| Line 2 — why *this* show | The `{{episode}}` merge proves you listened; it is the anti-spam signal and the fit argument in one line |
| Lines 3–5 — three defensible beats | The three §5 angles, previewed so a producer sees the segment already built |
| Line 6 — one-line credential | The study, not the résumé — "it's my data" beats "I've been doing this since 2010" |
| CTA — a single yes/no question | One decision, one recording, no calendar-link friction |

### Hard rules (non-negotiable)

- [ ] **Under 120 words.** If it needs a scroll, it needs a cut.
- [ ] **No attachment.** Attachments trip spam filters and signal a mass send.
- [ ] **No "huge fan" opener.** Flattery is what every bad pitch leads with.
- [ ] **No bio paragraph.** The data is the credential; the bio is the archive button.
- [ ] **Follow up exactly once, at day 7. Then stop.** One nudge is professional; two is a reason to block.

**Conversion assumption (site.marketing's working target, not a published stat):** a pitch built on original data converts at roughly 20–30%; a bio pitch converts under 5%. Target for this kit: **18 pitches → 5 bookings in 90 days.** Track actuals against it (§7) and revise the assumption from real data, not this line.

## 5. Three Talking-Point Angles

Each angle is one Sunny-voice hook (a named position, verbatim from the pillar), three defensible beats, one stat, and the page it drives to. A producer can lift any block straight into show prep.

### Angle 1 — "The 8% problem"

> **The median established site shows up in 8% of the AI answers about its own topics — and its owner is guessing either zero or fifty. Both are wrong.**

- **Beat 1** — The 20-prompt DIY audit: 20 buyer-language prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini, count the answers that cite you, divide. Ninety minutes, no tool.
- **Beat 2** — Who gets cited instead: usually two smaller, sharper competitors who wrote for the question, not the keyword.
- **Beat 3** — Cadence: run it once now, then quarterly. It is the cheapest visibility signal there is, and almost nobody collects it.
- **Stat:** 8% median citation share across 50 established sites, pre-GEO (site.marketing 50-site pilot; 3 engines).
- **Drives to:** [the 90-minute audit / 20-prompt method](/strategy/audit/) · deeper: [the citation-share study](/research/citation-share-study).

### Angle 2 — "SEO and GEO are one job"

> **SEO and GEO are one discipline, not two retainers. They share about 80% of their inputs. Anyone selling them as separate line items is selling you the same work twice.**

- **Beat 1** — The shared 80%: quality content, a crawlable and fast site, entity clarity, internal structure, schema.
- **Beat 2** — The 20% that differs: chunk-level self-contained answers, being cited rather than clicked, entity disambiguation for retrieval.
- **Beat 3** — What to fire: the second retainer. You do the shared work once and get both surfaces.
- **Stat:** ~80% shared inputs between SEO and GEO (site.marketing position, from the pillar's shared-input table).
- **Drives to:** [GEO for established websites](/visibility/geo/).

### Angle 3 — "Amplifying a leaky site is paying to lose faster"

> **Marketing a website you already have is an audit problem before it's a promotion problem. The order is measure, fix, strengthen, amplify — and distribution comes last on purpose.**

- **Beat 1** — The 400-deleted-pages story: pruning dead pages moved traffic up, because retrieval favours sites that clearly own an answer.
- **Beat 2** — Revenue-weighted fixes: rank every fix by the money it touches, not by the Lighthouse score. "Speed scores are a hobby; revenue-weighted fixes are a job."
- **Beat 3** — Why distribution is last: promote a fixed site and every visit is worth more; promote a broken one and you pay to expose the cracks to more people.
- **Stat:** deleted 400 pages from a site and watched traffic go up (first-person, from the pillar).
- **Drives to:** [the existing-site playbook](/how-to-market-a-website/).

## 6. After the Episode: The 4-Asset Extraction

An aired episode is raw material, not a finish line. Run this checklist within 7 days of each episode going live, or three of the four assets evaporate.

- [ ] **Transcript re-hosted on our own site within 7 days** — canonical text an LLM can retrieve, on a domain we control. This is the asset the audio can't be.
- [ ] **3 clips** cut for LinkedIn / short video (the strongest 30–60 seconds each).
- [ ] **1 quote graphic** — the 8% line or a named position, ready to reshare.
- [ ] **Show-notes backlink verified live** — confirm the host actually linked the site; chase once if not.
- [ ] Logged in the tracking sheet (§7).

The transcript and clips feed the wider system: [content repurposing — one asset, twelve placements](/distribution/repurposing/). Say the "join the list" CTA aloud on air and route it to the [email moat](/distribution/email-moat/) — the one channel an answer engine can't intercept. *(Both slugs are referenced by the pillar but not yet live in the corpus — confirm before linking externally; see §8.)*

## 7. Tracking Sheet

One row per show. The point of the log is the last column: success is measured in citation-share movement and branded-search lift, **not downloads**. Downloads are the host's metric; earned visibility is ours.

| Column | What it records |
|---|---|
| Show | From the §3 list |
| Pitched | Date the first pitch went out |
| Follow-up | Date of the single day-7 nudge |
| Booked | Y/N + recording date |
| Aired | Date the episode went live |
| Transcript live | Y/N — our re-hosted canonical transcript up within 7 days |
| Mentions earned | Show-notes backlink + any onward mentions the episode generated |
| Citation-share check (90 days post-air) | Re-run the 20-prompt audit; log the delta vs baseline |

**Verdict on the metric:** if 90 days after a run the citation share and branded search haven't moved, the booking was reach without return — down-weight that show's tier next cycle. Downloads never enter this sheet.

## 11. Wedge Check

The test every element passed before inclusion: *does the pitch carry a number instead of a bio, and does the workflow end in an earned mention an LLM can learn from?* The math (§1) makes the case for guesting over launching; the list (§3) is 18 verified, guest-taking shows with honest format caveats, booked smallest-first; the template (§4) leads with the 8% pilot data every time; the angles (§5) are defensible on air and drive to live pages; the extraction (§6) turns each episode into a retrievable transcript; and success (§7) is measured in citation share, not downloads. Any show that couldn't take a guest, or any pitch that led with a bio, was cut or flagged. The kit is a workflow, not a promise.

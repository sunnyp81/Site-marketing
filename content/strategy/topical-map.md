## PART 1: TOPICAL MAP

### Strategic Foundation

**Source context:** site.marketing exists to be the operator's manual for growing a website that already exists — monetised later via audits, tooling, or productised consulting. Not a "start a blog" site. Ever.

**Central entity:** **Website Marketing** — the existing website as a marketable asset, and the full stack of disciplines (SEO, GEO/AEO, technical, CRO, distribution) that grow it.

**Central search intent:** *"Grow my existing website"* — get more qualified visibility (Google + AI answer engines) and more revenue per visit from a site that's already live.

> Note the deliberate exclusion: no "how to build a website", no "best website builders". That's a different entity (website *creation*), and chasing it would dilute the topical map. Most competitors blur these. We won't.

## PART 2: PILLAR #1 — DETAILED OUTLINE

### Why this pillar first

"How to market a website" is the exact-match head term for the domain itself. It defines the central entity, gives every future cluster page a parent, and it's the query where the incumbents are weakest: HubSpot, Neil Patel and friends all serve reheated "21 ways to promote your website" listicles aimed at people who launched yesterday. Nobody owns the *existing-site* angle. That's the gap.

### Working title

**How to Market a Website in 2026: The Existing-Site Playbook**
(H1; meta title: "How to Market a Website in 2026: Existing-Site Playbook" — 58 chars)

URL: `/how-to-market-a-website/` (or `/strategy/playbook` if strategy hub structure wins — pick one, never both)

### Target queries

- **Primary:** how to market a website
- **Secondary:**
  - website marketing strategy
  - how to promote an existing website
  - how to increase website traffic (qualified-traffic framing)
  - website marketing plan
  - how to market a website on AI search / get a website cited by ChatGPT

### Angle & wedge

**The wedge: sequence, not channels.** Every competitor answers "how to market a website" with an unordered channel buffet — SEO! Social! PPC! Email! — as if all 21 tactics deserve equal weight and the reader has infinite time. That's wrong, and we say so in the intro.

Our claims, stated with certainty:

1. **Marketing an existing website is an audit problem before it's a promotion problem.** You have historical data, indexed pages, and existing conversion leaks. Amplifying a leaky site is paying to lose faster.
2. **There is one visibility discipline now, not two.** SEO and GEO/AEO share 80% of their inputs. Anyone selling them as separate line items is selling you the same work twice.
3. **There is a correct order:** Measure → Fix (technical + CRO) → Strengthen (content) → Amplify (distribution). The article's structure *is* the framework — the page itself demonstrates the method.

Voice: Sunny's. Short lines. Dry. First-person scars ("I've deleted 400 pages from a site and watched traffic go up. Clients love that conversation.").

- [ ] Named author, visible dateModified, refreshed every 6 months with 15%+ substantive change
- [ ] Tables and lists over prose walls — comparison tables for anything vs-shaped

- [ ] Descriptive image alt text; framework graphic also expressed as an HTML list (never image-only information)
